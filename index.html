University course preferences in Australia

Changing Course: What Australians Choose to Study

What do Australians want to study at university, and how has that changed? This page explores applications and enrolments across fields of study, from health and engineering to business, science and the arts.

By [Gan Jia Hui] · October 2026

Every year, hundreds of thousands of Australians apply to study at university. The courses they choose shape the skills of the future workforce and influence how universities grow.

This page looks at university course preferences from four angles: what students study today, how their interests have changed, which fields have grown fastest, and how the mix of fields differs across Australia. It uses official data from the Australian Government Department of Education.

Hover over any chart to see exact numbers.

Section 1

Fields of Study Today

How Australia's 1.7 million university students are spread across fields of study.

Business, Society & Culture and Health together account for more than half of all university students.

The share of international students ranges from 18% in Health to 65% in IT.

Regional applicants are more likely to choose Health and Education; city applicants are more likely to choose Business and IT.

Section 2

Changing Interests

How the popularity of each field has changed over the past decade.

Nine of the ten fields had more students in 2024 than in 2015; Business is the exception.

Health has been applicants' top choice since 2014, while Science moved up and Creative Arts moved down.

Almost every field shrank in 2022, during the COVID-19 border closures, before most grew again by 2024.

Section 3

Fastest-Growing Fields

Which fields have grown the most, among both students and applicants.

IT grew the most (+171%), followed by Health (+34%) and Architecture (+32%).

Applicants grew most for IT (+59%) and Science (+50%), and fell most for Creative Arts (−39%).

Section 4

Across Australia

How the mix of fields differs between states, territories and universities.

Health makes up 41% of students in Tasmania but 12% in New South Wales. Use the menu to compare any field.

New South Wales and Victoria have the most students; Business is the largest field in three states, Health in four, and Society & Culture in the Australian Capital Territory.

Most of the largest universities are in Sydney and Melbourne, and Health is the largest field at 16 of the 42.

Summary

Business, Society & Culture and Health remain Australia's largest fields of study. Health is the most popular choice among applicants, IT has grown fastest, and Creative Arts has attracted fewer applicants over time. The mix of fields also varies between states, between city and regional applicants, and between domestic and international students.

Notes on the data

  • Applicants (charts 3, 5 and 8) are domestic undergraduate applicants for semester 1 Commonwealth supported places, as at 15 May each year. They exclude international and postgraduate applicants. The way this data is collected changed in 2022.
  • City and regional applicants (chart 3) are the department's metropolitan and non-metropolitan applicants.
  • Students (all other charts) are all domestic and international students at all levels. Students in combined degrees are counted in both fields, so field shares are calculated from the sum of the ten broad fields.
  • State figures (charts 9 and 10) count students at universities and other providers based in each state, including students studying online. Providers that operate in several states are excluded. In chart 10, each circle is placed on its state and nudged slightly where neighbouring circles would touch.
  • University circles (chart 11) are placed at each university's main campus. The University of Notre Dame's 2024 figures may be affected by a cyber-security incident.
  • Society & Culture includes law, psychology, languages and economics.
  • The original Excel files and the CSV tables used by each chart are in this site's data folder.
